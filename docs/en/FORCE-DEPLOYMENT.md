# Force deployment

**English** | [Tiếng Việt](../vi/FORCE-DEPLOYMENT.md) | [简体中文](../zh-CN/FORCE-DEPLOYMENT.md)

Current game guidance, reviewed on **11 October 2026**. These formations explain roles and control choices, not exact historical deployments. Every age III example has **30 combat units**, excluding villagers, traders and Roman engineers. The examples are **not equal in cost or proven equal in strength**; adapt them to your economy, terrain, vision and opponent.

## Shared principles

Keep infantry/spears in front, ranged units and artillery behind, cavalry on the flanks, and a reserve near the retreat route or trade line. Leave space between groups to reduce enemy splash damage. Give large units and newly recruited units clear routes; do not rally everything into a narrow gap.

Build drop-offs near resources and protect the delivery paths. Trade runs between **two of your own markets**; distance raises gold per trip but also travel time and exposure. Do not move a busy drop-off camp without planning where villagers will deliver next.

## Rome

Age I: grow the economy, guard villagers with spears and scout routes. Age II: put legionaries/swords in front, spears on the flanks and archers behind; send Testudo only with escorts. Age III: add Onagers and engineers, then advance between safe rally points. Face shields toward incoming attacks; shields protect the individual legionary, not every unit behind it. Against cavalry, keep spears close; against artillery, spread out and flank with cavalry. Withdraw the slow siege engines first.

## Mongols

Age I: scout resources and routes, keep spears near gathering areas. Age II: use horse archers and raiders to threaten the economy from multiple directions; there is no heavy siege yet. Fire, then reposition before melee closes in; avoid frontal charges into spears. Age III: use lancers where there is room to build a charge, and escort the slow trebuchets. Its 9.5-tile nominal range helps against shorter-range artillery, but does not replace protection. Unpack moved camps before villagers need to deliver cargo.

## Persia

Age I: use durable spears to secure gathering and prepare food/gold. Age II: guards/swords hold the front, archers fire behind, and spears protect the ram. Age III: add guard archers, elephants and stone throwers according to budget. Keep volley archers out of melee; send elephants toward buildings or weakly defended flanks, never alone into dense spears. Cavalry can rescue the rear or raid enemy ranged units. Protect the two-market trade route, and do not recruit six elephants merely because the cap allows it.

## Tang China

Age I: guard resources with spears and scout approaches. Age II: swords/vanguard protect archers and the early traction trebuchet. Age III: add armored cavalry for flanking; the existing traction engine keeps its identity. Choose armory mode before placing new military orders. Use fast recruitment for replacement and pressure, quality gear when preparing a defensive line. The engine has nominal range 6.5 versus the Mongol trebuchet's 9.5, so reposition or flank instead of taking a stationary range duel. Keep armored cavalry away from dense spears and Ottoman gunfire.

## Ottomans

Age I: spears guard villagers and scouts observe routes. Age II: swords/spears and archers cover the transition to musketeers. Age III: build the cannon foundry and deploy cannons behind escorts, with a clear escape path. Spears stop cavalry while swords engage enemies reaching the guns. Split musketeers into groups and cover their roughly 3.3-second reloads. Move cannon first when retreating. Cannon nominal range is 8.5, below Mongol trebuchets; change position or raid their crews rather than assuming firepower wins every duel.

## Đại Việt

Age I: spears protect villagers while scouts find routes/resources. Age II: guards/spears form the front and bows/crossbows stay behind. Mobilize militia for nearby threats, accounting for equipment cost, travel and lost production; they do not replace a standing army. Age III: protect guns/cannon with infantry, use elephants where the enemy lacks spears, and counterattack when enemy lines spread out. Cannon range is 7 and splash is small, with no deployment mode. Demobilize reserves after the attack to resume valid previous work; conversion preserves HP percentage.

## Control the lines separately

Use separate groups for infantry, spears, ranged units, cavalry, siege and reserves. Ctrl/Cmd + 1–9 assigns groups; numbers select them, double-pressing focuses the camera. Right-click ground issues movement that interrupts combat until arrival. **A + click** engages enemies along the route; **D + click** issues retreat. Shift queues orders, S stops, H toggles hold position, and F focuses selection or your town center. Shields and deployment use their own buttons and do not activate just because a unit is in the suggested position.

## Counter limits

- Spear/vanguard bonuses depend on the target's game role; horse archers are ranged, so they do not receive the melee-cavalry ×3 spear multiplier.
- Siege ranged attacks have no minimum firing distance yet. Their weaknesses are HP, armor, speed and reload time.
- Frontal shields protect their owner; armor penetration does not bypass shield reduction.
- Splash damages enemies, with no friendly splash damage. Spread out against opposing artillery.
- Front/rear lines are player-organized orders, not a formation lock. AI does not yet use all civilization skills.
