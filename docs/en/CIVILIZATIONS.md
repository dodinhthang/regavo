# Civilizations and unit rosters

**English** | [Tiếng Việt](../vi/CIVILIZATIONS.md) | [简体中文](../zh-CN/CIVILIZATIONS.md)

Current game rules, reviewed on **11 October 2026**. The six civilizations draw on different historical periods. The three ages are gameplay progression, not a shared chronology. Unit statistics, team colors, recruitment gates and caps are game design choices.

Each roster below lists all types available by age III, including civilian and mobilized units. One trader with its pack animal, one elephant with riders, or one siege engine with crew counts as one population slot. Every side has a population cap of **200**. Historical sources guide inspiration; images from linked research websites are not included as game assets.

## Rome

Legionaries can toggle a directional shield: incoming frontal damage is reduced by 55% before armor, while movement speed falls by 45%. Engineers build/repair twice as fast as villagers. Connected outposts support construction/repair near their network; outposts do not recruit. The age II Testudo ram and age III torsion Onager have different jobs and require escorts. Traders have light armor.

## Mongols

Horse archers are mobile ranged units; lancers gain ×1.7 damage after at least four tiles of accumulated movement. From age II, lumber camps, mining camps and granaries can pack and move; they must unpack on valid land to receive cargo. There is no age II ram or heavy siege engine. Age III opens the counterweight trebuchet. Traders are fast, cheap and fragile.

## Persia

Guards hold the line and guard archers fire three-arrow volleys. Age III elephants cost 200 food + 140 gold and take 45 seconds; the cap of six includes active and queued elephants. Trade gold per trip is multiplied by 1.3 before rounding. Age II has the covered ram; age III adds a stone-throwing siege engine. This broad Persian visual identity is not restricted to one dynasty.

## Tang China

The age II traction trebuchet is pulled by people; it does not become a counterweight machine in age III. The armory applies a mode to newly placed military orders: fast recruitment takes 30% less time; quality equipment gives +10% HP and +2 armor but takes 15% longer. Mode changes take 10 seconds and do not retrofit existing units or earlier orders. Traders are cheaper than the baseline.

## Ottomans

Musketeers open in age II, penetrate 50% of armor and receive ×1.25 damage against melee cavalry. The age III cannon is recruited at the cannon foundry. Deployment reduces its five-second firing interval to 3.25 seconds; moving cancels deployment. The generic siege workshop and ram/stone-thrower recruitment are unavailable. Traders are tougher and slower than the baseline.

## Đại Việt mobilization and combat limits

From age II, select villagers and choose **Mobilize militia**. Each costs **20 food + 10 wood**. They deliver existing cargo before travelling to a completed friendly barracks, then train for **8 seconds**. If no drop-off exists, they wait for one. Demobilization is free but requires returning to a barracks and another **8 seconds**; it refunds no used equipment.

Conversion retains ID, population and HP percentage; it does not heal. Villagers stop working during conversion and remain vulnerable. A new order, Stop/Hold, or loss of the barracks cancels conversion: before training the full mobilization cost is refunded, during training the unused `remaining/8` fraction is refunded. Death refunds nothing. Saved work resumes only if its target is valid. Active, mobilizing and demobilizing militia share a cap of **12**; active and queued elephants share a cap of **6**.

AI mobilizes only for visible enemies within 18 tiles of its town center, keeps at least ten economic villagers, and uses at most 2/6/8 reserves on Easy/Normal/Hard. It demobilizes after 30 seconds without that local threat.

Militia have 65 HP, 7 damage, no armor and ×2 damage against cavalry/elephants. Crossbowmen have 55 HP, 12 damage, 5.5 range, a 2-second interval and ×1.15 against infantry. Đại Việt elephants have 380 HP, 22 damage and 2 armor, costing 170 food + 110 gold and 38 seconds. Handgunners penetrate 35% armor. Cannon range is 7, splash radius 0.8 and building multiplier ×2.2; Đại Việt cannon have no Ottoman deployment mode. These tradeoffs do not certify faction balance.

## How to read the references

Persian art combines broader influences than the Achaemenid concept boards; Sasanian siege/elephant references do not identify every illustrated machine exactly. Mongol counterweight siege represents adoption of technology, not a claim of invention. The surviving Dardanelles cannon is dated 1464; it is not the actual gun used in 1453. See [Metropolitan Museum Journal 24, p. 193](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Model_of_a_Basilisk_by_Petrus_de_Arena_The_Metropolitan_Museum_Journal_v_24_1989.pdf). Đại Việt is represented on land; the absence of a navy here does not describe its historical capabilities. Uniforms, transport animals and symbols are artistic interpretations.
